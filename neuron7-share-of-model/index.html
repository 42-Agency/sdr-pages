01 — TWO METRICS

Brand awareness is not buying AI recommendations

These are two separate games. Right = more people Google the brand. Up = AI recommends it more often. Winning one does not win the other: ServiceMax has eight times Neuron7's search volume, almost all of it existing customers logging in. SupportLogic has real search volume and zero AI recommendations. Neuron7 is second among the specialist vendors on both measures, behind Aquant.

02 — SHARE OF MODEL

Who AI recommends — 16 questions × 4 engines

The shortlist AI hands buyers is dominated by the platforms they already own — Salesforce, Microsoft, ServiceMax, IFS, ServiceNow. Among the specialists it is a two-horse race: Aquant first, Neuron7 second, and nobody else close. SightCall, Makula, Aiventic, Bruviti and CareAR barely register, and SupportLogic never comes up at all.

The four numbers beside each bar: how many of the 16 questions each engine answered with that brand. Vendors marked * are the big platforms, not specialists — AI recommends them for the same questions, so they are counted.

THE GEMINI GAP

The problem is concentrated in one engine. Gemini — the AI inside Google — mentions Neuron7 on 1 question out of 16. It mentions Aquant on 11. A buyer who asks Google's AI sees a market in which Neuron7 barely exists. Perplexity is the opposite: Neuron7 on 7 of 16, more than any competitor's engine coverage of it.

03 — WHAT AI READS

Why Neuron7 is missing: AI copies its shortlist from a few web pages

AI doesn't know this market. It reads a handful of web pages and repeats what they say. Every answer told us which pages it read, and we logged all of them. Most of the influential pages are published by vendors — so the vendors who publish the most get recommended the most. Aquant's sites were read in 10 of the 64 answers. Neuron7's in 7.

Most-cited sources across the 64 answers

Each source counted once per answer. Gemini hides which sites it read, so its sources are left out rather than guessed at.

A COMPETITOR'S BLOG POST IS PICKING THE SHORTLIST

The second most-read source is truecontext.com — a competing vendor. Its "best AI field service platforms" article ranks itself first and Neuron7 fifth, and the engines repeat that ranking. Right now, one competitor blog post has more influence over who AI recommends than Neuron7's entire website.

THE BIGGEST QUESTIONS HAVE NO ANSWER YET

Asked "how do we keep expert technicians' knowledge before they retire" — the problem Neuron7 leads its homepage with — no engine named any vendor. Not Neuron7, not Aquant, nobody. The same is true of the questions about fixing faster: one vendor mention across eight answers. These questions are not lost to a competitor. They are unclaimed.

ONE QUESTION WENT TO THE WRONG INDUSTRY

"Software that predicts equipment failures" was answered with sensor companies — Augury, IBM Maximo, Siemens Senseye. AI hears that phrase as a hardware problem. Neuron7 makes the same promise with software, and the engines don't make the connection.

The questions Neuron7 is missing from are not owned by anyone. Aquant appears in 2 of those 20 answers, Neuron7 in 0. Whoever publishes credible pages against them first takes them.

04 — WHERE NEURON7 DISAPPEARS

All four engines draw the line in the same place

We grouped the 16 questions by how the buyer asked them. Each cell shows how many of that group's questions the engine answered with Neuron7. Four engines, asked independently, split the same way: Neuron7 exists when the buyer uses industry vocabulary, and vanishes when the buyer describes pain.

13/28 · BUYER USES INDUSTRY VOCABULARY

When the buyer already knows what this is called

If the question contains a product category or a competitor's name, Neuron7 appears in about half the answers — and the engines describe it correctly: complex equipment, Fortune 1000 scale, medical devices and industrial manufacturers. Its best questions are "alternatives to Aquant" and "alternatives to ServiceMax."

0/20 · BUYER DESCRIBES THE PAIN

When the buyer describes the problem

The five problem questions are, almost word for word, the promises on Neuron7's homepage: keep expert knowledge, fix issues faster, prevent downtime, ramp new technicians. Zero mentions in 20 answers. The buyers earliest in their journey — the ones who only know their pain — never hear the name.

The engines already know the answer. They just never give it.

Ask Gemini to compare Neuron7 and Aquant by name and it says: "Choose Neuron7 if your primary bottleneck is complex troubleshooting, low first-time fix rates and long onboarding cycles." Ask the same engine what improves first-time fix rate — without naming anyone — and Neuron7 is not in the answer. The knowledge is in the engine. It only surfaces when the buyer already knows the name, because the pages the engine reads for the problem questions never mention Neuron7.

Who gets those answers instead: first-time fix goes to Salesforce and SightCall, new-technician ramp to Microsoft and Salesforce, failure prediction to the sensor companies. Knowledge capture goes to nobody.

05 — BRANDED SEARCH

Fewer people Google Neuron7 than Aquant — but only Neuron7 is growing

Real people Googling each brand in the US over the past 12 months. Neuron7 gets 490 searches a month to Aquant's 1,030. The direction matters more than the gap: Neuron7 climbed steadily all year, SupportLogic fell 17%, ServiceMax drifted down, and Aquant was flat outside one news spike. Neuron7 is the only brand in this market whose search demand is growing.

06 — WHEN NEURON7 IS NAMED

The engines rate you highly. They just rarely bring you up.

We also asked all four engines about Neuron7 directly — is it good, how does it compare to Aquant, does it integrate. When the engines are asked by name, they recommend it. The problem is not reputation. It is that buyers who don't know the name never get it.

ALL 4 ENGINES AGREE

Unprompted endorsement

ChatGPT: "Yes — Neuron7 is worth shortlisting for enterprise field service, especially when the main challenge is diagnosing and repairing complex equipment." Gemini: "widely considered one of the stronger, purpose-built AI platforms for enterprise field service." Perplexity: "a strong fit for large enterprise field-service organizations." Gemini volunteers the customer list on its own: Ciena, TransLogic, Keysight, Terumo BCT.

HEAD-TO-HEAD VS AQUANT

Engines give Neuron7 the hard problems

Asked to choose between the two, the engines split the market the same way: "Choose Neuron7 if your bottleneck is complex troubleshooting, low first-time fix rates and long onboarding" (Gemini); Aquant gets workforce analytics and cost dashboards. Perplexity hands Neuron7 the large-enterprise column outright: "it targets Fortune 1000 organizations, commonly with 1,000+ technicians."

THE ONE SOFT SPOT

The proof is self-sourced

Claude endorses the product but flags the evidence: "much of the evidence comes from the vendor and affiliated reviewers." The 3-hours-to-3-seconds and 46%-faster numbers engines quote all trace back to neuron7.ai itself. Independent, third-party versions of those proofs don't exist yet for engines to find.

07 — METHODOLOGY

Exactly what we did

SHARE OF MODEL

21 prompts × 4 engines = 84 answers

16 category questions (counted) + 5 questions naming Neuron7 (read separately), each put to all four engines. Every engine ran its current production model with live web search on, so every answer is sourced and the sources were captured. No counted question names Neuron7. One pass per engine; all 84 answers returned.

SCORING

Mention tally

A brand scores once per answer however often it is named, so this counts how widely it is recommended, not how much ink it gets. A question's own subject does not score (Aquant in "alternatives to Aquant", Salesforce in the Salesforce question). The brand list was built from the answers, not written in advance. Citation URLs are stripped first, so a vendor that only appears in a link does not score. 279 mentions, 36 vendors, 64 answers.

SHARE OF SEARCH

Branded search demand

Branded Google volume (US, 12-month average) across 8 brands × 15 modifiers, with spelling variants that Google counts as the same query removed before summing. Ambiguous brand words modelled from their unambiguous queries and reported as a range. Growth read from the month-by-month series, not the average.
