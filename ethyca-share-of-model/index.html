08 — WHAT TO DO

Four moves, in priority order

1

Rewrite the comparison guides so they argue for Ethyca

Highest leverage in the report: the page is already the most-read one Ethyca has. Then build the ones that don't exist — engines are being asked for alternatives to BigID, Securiti and Collibra too.

2

Publish AI-governance guides in the buyer's words

The seven questions scoring 1 of 28 are ones Ethyca is uniquely qualified to answer: enforcing policy at the point of access, governing what agents do with production data, personal data in retrieval pipelines. Guides, not product pages.

3

Lead with Fides

The one thing all four engines agree Ethyca is for. Treat it as the top of funnel it already is, and make the path from Fides to Astralis explicit in what the engines read.

4

Decide what Astralis is for, given nobody will search it

A name with zero volume whose token belongs to an esports team won't become a search asset. Fine if Astralis is for sales conversations and Ethyca carries demand. A problem if the plan assumed otherwise.

One blind spot. Ethyca's Search Console isn't connected, so there's no first-party view of which queries surface ethyca.com and which get clicked. It's the one surface that would show whether the guides are found on category terms or only by name. Cheapest gap to close before the next run.

09 — METHOD

Method

What we ran

16 questions in the buyer's language, none naming Ethyca, put to ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity with live search on — 64 answers. Framing was varied deliberately: category, role, problem. A further 5 questions name Ethyca; those are read qualitatively and excluded from every count here. A brand scores once per answer, so this measures breadth, not word count. 83 brands were named; the vocabulary came from the answers, not a list we brought.

Why not a survey

Brand recall needs a representative panel of your buyer, which doesn't exist at workable cost for this category. Behavioural measurement sidesteps it — anyone searching or asking has selected themselves in. The trade-off: it sees buyers in motion, so it under-counts dormant awareness. A complement to a thin attitudinal survey, not a replacement.

How the numbers were checked

Every answer tallied twice by two independently written counters and compared. They disagreed on one of 64; the raw text showed the second counter had been fooled by a model's planning notes and a citation link. Fixed, they agree on all 64 answers and all 381 mentions. Brands whose names are ordinary words were inspected by hand. The 13 answers naming no vendor were read in full to confirm they were genuine.

Branded search

19 brands × 14 fixed buying-intent phrases, US, 12-month average. Common-word brands are modelled, not taken at face value: "transcend", "ketch" and "private ai" collect searches meant for other things, and counting them raw handed one vendor 39% of the category. Every zero was re-pulled in a different batch to confirm it.

What we didn't claim

No 12-month momentum trend. Every brand and the category phrase itself appeared to drop 35–58% recently, because Google withholds weeks below a reporting threshold and that drags the average down for exactly the smaller brands in question. The five-year view is the only defensible read, and for Ethyca even that is too sparse.

This is the baseline

June 2026 used two engines and a different question set, so it isn't comparable and isn't mixed in. It found the same shape. Re-run these 21 questions on the same four engines quarterly; compare per-engine counts out of 16 and pooled out of 64, never across a different number of engines.
