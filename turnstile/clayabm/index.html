The diagnosis

Spreadsheet finance was a feature when ARR was small. It is the bottleneck now.

Three structural causes show up at the same time in usage-based SaaS at this scale. None of them are unique to one company. All three are inevitable if the system of record is a spreadsheet.

01

Pricing is product. The model ships weekly.

Credit packs, hybrid tiers, AI overages, enterprise carve-outs. The pricing surface area moves faster than any billing tool was scoped for. Every release adds another exception that finance handles by hand.

02

Quote terms become code, then become bugs.

Custom rates, ramps, mid-cycle upsells, overage true-ups. Each one is a one-off in CPQ, then a manual mirror in the ERP. Each manual mirror is a future reconciliation ticket.

03

ARR is a derived metric, not a stored one.

Without a system that ties signed quote to subscription state to invoice to recognition, ARR is whatever Excel computed this morning. Two ops people, three sources of truth, no audit trail.

What we'd change

Stop guessing at ARR. Start storing it.

The shift is mechanical, not philosophical. Four moves, in order. Each one removes a manual handoff and a place where the number can drift.

1

Move the quote out of Google Docs.

The signed quote is the contract. The contract is the schedule. CRM-synced, e-sign built in, structured pricing fields the rest of the stack can read. A PDF is not a system of record.

2

Tie subscription state to the signed quote.

Mid-cycle upsell becomes an amendment, not a new spreadsheet line. Downgrades, terminations, ramp-ups, and overages flow from one object that already knows the terms.

3

Generate invoices from subscription state.

Not from a CSV pulled out of Stripe. Prorations, ramps, and usage charges are derived in real time. Mid-cycle changes invoice on the next cycle, accurately, without anyone touching them.

4

Recognize revenue from the invoice.

Not from a spreadsheet that finance updates on Fridays. ARR, MRR, bookings, deferred — all derived from the same chain. Audit-ready by construction. The number you tell investors is the number in the system.
