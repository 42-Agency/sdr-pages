CONCEPT · MORNINGSTAR WORKED EXAMPLE
A BRAND-FIRST READING OF AI VISIBILITY ONE BRAND · MANY ARENAS · DIFFERENT COMPETITORS

A famous brand isn't one brand. It's a portfolio of capabilities — and AI remembers each one differently.

How a brand performs inside AI answer engines is a function of how strong that brand is in real life — per capability. So the readout shouldn't be one blended "AI visibility" score. It should be a capability map: rank the brand across the keywords each of its business units competes on, and in each arena the competitors are different. Where the model doesn't name you, the brand story for that audience is thin or under-activated — that's the gap to fix.

Below is a live worked example on Morningstar — a single trusted brand that plays in at least six arenas. We asked ChatGPT and Perplexity the buyer questions for each and counted who gets named. The verdict swings hard by capability: the model ranks Morningstar #1 in fund research, but #4 in credit ratings (behind Moody's, S&P, Fitch) and #5 in indexes. And in private markets it ranks #1 — as PitchBook — while 93% of those answers never mention Morningstar at all.

THE PRINCIPLE THIS READOUT IS BUILT ON

01 — THE REFRAME

Stop scoring the brand. Score the capability tree.

A blended "share of AI answers" hides the real picture. Morningstar's top keyword is investment ratings & research — but the brand also plays in credit ratings, ESG, private markets, indexes, and data platforms. Each is a separate arena with a different competitive set. The model's answer in each arena mirrors the brand's real strength there. Strong halo, uneven branches.

One brand → many arenas

Each business unit competes on its own capability keywords. "Credit ratings" ≠ "ESG ratings" ≠ "private market data." Measure them separately.

Different competitors each

In credit it's Moody's, S&P, Fitch. In ESG it's MSCI. In private markets it's Preqin & CB Insights. The rival set changes every branch.

AI rank = brand strength

Where the model names you first, the brand is activated for that audience. Where it forgets you, the story needs building — not an SEO patch.

02 — THE CAPABILITY MAP

Morningstar's share of AI answers, by capability

Each bar is one arena, ordered by Morningstar's strength. Width = Morningstar's share of answer prominence there; the badge is its rank among all named providers. The ghost marker is the arena's actual front-runner. Read top-to-bottom: where the halo carries, and where it doesn't.

03 — INSIDE EACH ARENA

Different arena, different competitors, different verdict

Each card is one capability. The competitive set is specific to that arena — this is what a brand agency actually briefs against. Morningstar's row is highlighted.

04 — WHERE THE EQUITY LEAKS

The model knows the product — but not that it's Morningstar

In several arenas Morningstar competes under an acquired sub-brand — DBRS in credit, Sustainalytics in ESG, PitchBook in private markets. When the assistants name the product without connecting it to Morningstar, the parent brand's equity leaks to the sub-brand. That's a brand-architecture decision, made visible.

05 — THE BRAND READ

What a brand agency does with this

DEFEND

Arenas where the model already leads with you are mental-availability assets. Protect the associations that put you there and keep feeding them.

ACTIVATE

Challenger arenas are a story-and-activation problem, not a schema problem. The capability is real; the brand narrative for that audience isn't landing yet.

DECIDE

Where equity leaks to a sub-brand, that's an architecture call: endorse harder ("Morningstar Sustainalytics") or let the sub-brand run free. Either way, choose it on purpose.

The capability map turns "our AI visibility is low" into a specific brief: which arena, which audience, which competitor is winning the association, and whether the fix is story, activation, or architecture.

06 — METHODOLOGY

Exactly what we did

Share of Model reads salience among the in-market — it observes who the answer engines actually name, not stated recall. LLM answers vary run-to-run; the pattern across a prompt set is stable, the decimal is not. Re-run the identical prompt set to track movement over time.
