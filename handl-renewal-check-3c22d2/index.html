In the first two pairings the hospital and the surgery center are the same organization. Emerson Endoscopy and Digestive Health Center is an Emerson Hospital joint venture about two miles from the hospital. Shields Surgery Center Medford is a Shields Health and MelroseWakefield joint venture, and MelroseWakefield is part of Tufts Medicine, which lists it among its own locations. Hospitals are not uniformly more expensive — a few in this market price below the surgery-center median, which is exactly why the comparison has to be run on your markets rather than assumed.

Why this matters at renewal: spreads like these are already inside the quote you're about to approve. Colonoscopy is the case where the cheaper site of service is already in-network, already accredited, and sometimes owned by the same hospital — so the gap isn't a quality trade-off. Plans pay it silently, employees burn deductibles on it, and nothing in the renewal paperwork shows you it exists.

Fair questions

What everyone asks first

Are you a broker?
No. Handl is not a broker, TPA, or carrier. We work alongside your broker, not around them. Many brokers use this same data with their clients; yours can too.
Is a surgery center really equivalent for this?
For a routine screening colonoscopy, ambulatory surgery centers are Medicare-certified, accredited, and staffed by the same gastroenterologists who practice at the hospital — in two of the pairings above they are literally the same organization. Patients with real clinical complexity belong in a hospital, and nothing here suggests otherwise.
Is any of this PHI?
No. The check starts from zip codes only. No names, no claims, no health information of any kind.
We're mid-cycle. Is it too late?
No. Nothing here touches your renewal itself, so the check is useful at any point in the cycle. Before the quote lands is simply when it's most useful.
Do we have to change carriers or networks?
No. The check is information about the plan you already have. What you do with it is your call.
