WHAT AI ANSWERS WHEN BUYERS ASK CHATGPT + PERPLEXITY + GEMINI · AUG 2026

Repario doesn't have an AI visibility problem. It has a positioning problem that AI happens to measure.

We asked ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini the 18 questions buyers ask when they're picking an eDiscovery provider. Across 54 answers the three engines named 104 companies. Repario wasn't one of them — and the lists weren't short, averaging nine names each, including firms much smaller than Repario.

Ask about Repario by name and all three engines answer accurately and well: the Chambers ranking, the project-management bench, the consultative model, the companies it was built from. They know Repario. They just never think of it.

HOW OFTEN THE THREE ENGINES NAME THE SAME COMPANIES

For each group of questions: of all the companies named, what share turned up in all three engines' answers. A high number means the market has an agreed answer and a challenger has to displace someone. A low number means the answer is still up for grabs.

The "who's biggest" question is taken

When buyers just ask who's best, Epiq appears in every one of the 12 answers and twelve brands turn up in half or more. The engines also agree with each other most here. Competing here means outspending Epiq, Relativity and Consilio on a reputation they already have.

Managed review is wide open

42 companies were named across nine managed-review answers and the engines agree on 6% of them — the lowest in the run. Two of those questions produced no shared name at all. Managed review is also something Repario is genuinely good at.

Nobody owns the service claim

Across the collaboration and service-model questions, no brand appears in more than 58% of answers, 41 different companies get named, and the three engines agree on 9% of them. Buyers ask this question and get a different answer every time.

06 — WHAT TO DO

What to do about it

PICK THE QUESTION

Skip "best eDiscovery provider" — Epiq already owns that one. Go after the working-alongside-your-team and managed-review questions, where there is no automatic answer and you win with a clear claim rather than a bigger budget.

SAY ONE THING

"Collaboration" is the right idea and the wrong word — too vague for anyone to repeat. The firms that won that question used a concrete promise a buyer could repeat back. Repario needs its version of that sentence.

MEASURE THE MOVE

This run is the baseline. Re-run the same 18 questions each quarter and brand work gets a number attached to it. Zero to named in a third of answers is something a board can read.

None of this is fixed by publishing more. Repario is already knowledgeable, already ranked, already capable. It has never told the market what it is in words the market repeats back.

07 — METHODOLOGY

Exactly what we did

The full prompt set
Three of the 54 answers named nobody

Asked for a "boutique provider with high-touch service," ChatGPT returned marketing-slogan suggestions rather than companies. Perplexity answered two questions — managed review providers, and AI document review — with a generic explainer naming no vendors. Gemini named companies for all 18. We kept the three empty answers in the count rather than deleting them, because an engine that names nobody is a real result: it means the question has no established answer in the category.

This measures what buyers who are actively looking get told — who the answer engines actually name, rather than what people say they remember in a survey. AI answers vary a little between runs, so treat the pattern as solid and the exact percentage as approximate; re-running the same questions is what shows movement.
