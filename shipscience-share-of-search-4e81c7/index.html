07 — WHAT WE'D DO FIRST

Four moves, in the order we'd make them

None needs new content. All four are checkable against the numbers above.

1

Consolidate the claim cluster and put a product path on it

37 claim pages, 15 of them on UPS claims, are splitting authority for queries worth 24,700 searches/mo. Pick one canonical destination per intent — file a UPS claim, file a FedEx claim, lost package, damaged package — redirect the rest into it, and give each a real route into SimpleClaims. Right now the #8 position is a page about a phone number with nothing to convert into. Highest-value hour in this report, and it costs no new words.

2

Write the problem-shaped pages — the ones the engines gave you 0/12 on

Three questions, zero mentions from four engines: overpaying carriers, packages arriving damaged, is UPS billing us correctly. Those are the same queries carrying 26,900 searches/mo at under $13 a click, and the same ones LateShipment ranks on. You have 3,677 pages about how carriers work and apparently none that answer "this is happening to me, what now." That's the one genuine content gap, and it's narrow — a handful of pages, not a programme.

3

Make the third-party proof reachable, and fix the stale directory slugs

All four engines discount your evidence as self-reported. You have 43 Capterra reviews at 4.9 — better than the vendors outranking you — and not one engine found it. Surface it where they look: on your own comparison pages, in the directory listings, and in a ShipScience-vs-Sifted page, which is the comparison all four engines already associate you with and the one Claude explicitly failed to find. While you're in the directories, the URL slugs still say refund-geeks and UPS-FedEx-Parcel-Audit — which is where Gemini's "formerly Refund Geeks" line comes from.

4

Stop steering by share of search, and find out what Sifted is doing

A 6,700/mo category is too thin a sample to manage against — monthly moves under a point are inside Google's rounding. Share of Model is the instrument that matters in a category where buyers ask for a shortlist, and it's now baselined: 4.6%, 15 of 64, with the cluster map above. Re-run the identical 16 questions quarterly and track the cluster shape, not the decimal. Separately, Sifted went 6.9% → 25.7% in nine months and holds #4 on the money query. Whatever is compounding there is the most informative thing in this category right now.

08 — METHOD & CAVEATS

Exactly what we did, and where it's soft

Run date 14 October 2026. United States, English, desktop. Reproducible from a browser.

SHARE OF MODEL

16 questions × 4 engines

ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity, each on its current production model with live web search on, one pass each. All 64 answers returned. No counted question names ShipScience. Four additional questions that do name it were read qualitatively and excluded from the share.

SCORING

One point per answer

A brand scores once per answer however often it's named, so this measures how widely it's recommended, not how much ink it gets. A question's own subject doesn't score (Sifted in "alternatives to Sifted"). The 41-brand vocabulary was harvested from the answers, not written in advance. Citation URLs are stripped first, so a vendor appearing only in a link doesn't score.

381 branded queries, 18 vendors

Each brand's stem crossed with 19 modifiers — bare, pricing, reviews, alternatives, vs, demo, login, cost, competitors, software, app, careers, plus seven category qualifiers. Branded Google volume, US, with all 12 monthly data points kept so the trend is readable rather than a single average.

Where we'd argue with our own numbers

  1. Sifted and Loop are modelled, not measured. Their names are ordinary words. Sifted's defensible floor is 50/mo; the 760 figure attributes 2.6% of a 27,680/mo generic pool dominated by a European startup-news site and the cooking verb. A one-point shift in that attribution moves Sifted by ±270. Loop is worse — 0.1% attribution against loop earplugs at 165,000/mo. Their ranks are directional. Sifted's trend is the part we'd defend.
  2. Intelligent Audit's branded number is the weakest in section 02. 720 of its 830 monthly searches sit on the bare phrase intelligent audit, which is also a generic concept query. We counted it as branded. If half of it isn't, it drops below Sifted. Worth a SERP check before using that figure competitively.
  3. We corrected a double-count mid-run; it moved the leaderboard. Google normalises spelling variants, so intelligentaudit and intelligent audit were the same query reported twice — as were ShipMatrix's and Share a Refund's variants. Before the fix Intelligent Audit led at 20.5% and the category looked 12% larger. It is now 2nd. ShipScience's rank was unaffected either way.
  4. ship science (110/mo) is folded into ShipScience. Some of it is literal naval-architecture search. Your hard floor is 220/mo and 3.3% share; 4.9% is the generous read of your own number.
  5. SERP positions are one capture, one day, one location. ups claim returned both #4 and #8 for ShipScience across two pulls minutes apart with identical settings, and the surrounding results differed too. Treat any single position as a band. The pattern across six queries is what holds.
  6. AI answers vary run to run. One pass per engine. We'd trust the 0/12 and the 4/4 — those are unanimous across four independently-prompted models — and treat the gap between 4.6% and Loop's or TransImpact's 4.9% as a tie. Re-running the identical set is what makes this a tracker rather than a snapshot.
  7. Three AI mentions were manual false positives we removed. "Loop" was being credited for Loop Returns, a returns-management vendor, and once for the ordinary noun. Every ambiguous brand's matches were read in context before counting.
  8. Gemini hides its publishers. It wraps citations in a Google redirect that conceals the source, so those 12 are excluded from the 121-domain count rather than guessed at. Every other engine's citations resolve to a real domain.
  9. The Capterra figures come from its live category page. Rating and review counts as published on 14 Oct 2026. The directory URL slugs are visible in Google's index; we could not load those listing pages directly.
  10. US only. If you sell into Canada or the EU this understates both your demand and the category's.
  11. Search volumes are bucketed. Google rounds to steps — 10, 40, 90, 210, 390 — visible in the monthly series. Small month-to-month moves are rounding, not behaviour.
