WHY THIS IS THE USEFUL COMPARISON

LateShipment and ShipScience are the two vendors in this set built around the same insight — that the recoverable money is in failed deliveries, not in contract rates. They have made opposite bets on where to spend. LateShipment put its weight on owning the refund and claim SERPs and let the brand stay small. ShipScience built four times the brand and a far larger content library, and sits behind them on the pages where that library should be paying out.

That's not a content deficit, it's a consolidation deficit. 15 separate UPS-claim pages competing with each other for one query is a predictable way to rank #8 instead of #2. The fix is structural and it is cheap relative to writing anything new.

06 — WHAT WE'D DO FIRST

Three moves, in the order we'd make them

None of these require new content. All three are checkable against the numbers above.

1

Consolidate the claim cluster, then put a product path on it

15 UPS-claim pages and 37 claim pages are splitting the authority for queries worth 24,700 searches/mo. SourceForge, GetApp, Capterra, SendCloud, Freehand, Hatfield and Intek hold roughly a third of the top-10 positions across the six commercial queries. You will not beat a software directory for best parcel audit software, and you don't need to — you need to be in it. Reveel, Loop, Intelligent Audit and Betachon already are. This is a listings-and-outreach task measured in weeks, not a content programme measured in quarters.

3

Stop measuring brand with share of search alone, and find out what Sifted is doing

A 7,500/mo category gives you a sample too thin to steer by — month-to-month moves under a point are inside the rounding. The instrument that actually matters in a category where buyers ask for a shortlist is how often you get named when someone asks an AI engine for the best parcel audit platform, measured against the same 18 vendors. Separately: Sifted nearly quadrupled its share in nine months and holds #4 on the money query. Whatever is driving that is the most informative thing in this category right now, and it is knowable.

AMBIGUITY HANDLING

Three brands are common words

Loop, Sifted and Reveel share their names with unrelated things. Category-qualified modifiers are counted in full; the generic pool is discounted by the ratio their disambiguated siblings imply. Without this, loop earplugs (165,000/mo) and sifted flour (1,300/mo) get banked as parcel-audit demand. Reported levels for those three are modelled, not measured.

Where we'd argue with our own numbers

  1. Intelligent Audit's #1 position is the weakest figure in the report. 720 of its 830 monthly searches sit on the bare phrase intelligent audit, which is also a generic concept query. We counted it as branded. If half of it isn't, their lead over TransImpact largely closes. We'd want a SERP check before anyone used that ranking competitively.
  2. Sifted and Loop are modelled, not measured. Sifted's defensible floor is 50/mo; the 760 figure attributes 2.6% of a 27,680/mo generic pool dominated by a European startup-news site and the cooking verb. A one-point shift in that attribution moves Sifted by ±270. Loop is worse. Their ranks are directional; Sifted's trend is the part we'd defend.
  3. ship science (110/mo) is folded into ShipScience. Some of it is literal naval-architecture search. The hard floor is 220/mo and 2.9% share; 4.4% is the generous read of your own number.
  4. SERP positions are one capture on one day from one location. We saw ups claim return both #4 and #8 for ShipScience across two pulls hours apart — same day, same settings. Treat single positions as a band, not a point. The pattern across six queries is what holds; any individual rank might move.
  5. US only. If you sell into Canada or the EU this understates both your brand demand and the category's.
  6. Search volumes are bucketed. Google rounds to steps — 10, 40, 90, 210, 390 — which is visible in the monthly series. Small month-to-month moves are quantisation, not behaviour.
