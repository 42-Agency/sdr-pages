01 — TWO METRICS

Share of Model vs Share of Search

Share of Model = how often a brand is named when a buyer asks an AI engine to recommend a vendor. Share of Search = branded Google demand. Each dot is a vendor: up = recommended more by AI, right = searched more by buyers. This category splits violently. The HR suites (Culture Amp, 15Five, Degreed) own enormous branded search and are barely recommended for coaching. The coaching specialists — Valence, Torch, CoachHub — live in the top-left: strong AI authority, almost no search volume.

This is a good position to be in — Valence is recommended far above its brand size. It also means the AI layer is doing nearly all the discovery work, because there is almost no branded search to fall back on. Which makes Share of Model the KPI that matters here, not search volume.

02 — SHARE OF MODEL

Who AI recommends — 16 unbranded category questions

Share of the 93 brand mentions across 16 answers. Valence places 4th at 7.5%, inside a tight leading pack. The notable feature isn't the gap to BetterUp — it's that 37 distinct vendors were named across only 16 questions. This category has no consensus answer, which means the recommendation set is still genuinely winnable.

Microsoft Viva, Microsoft Copilot, Workday, ServiceNow and the LMS vendors (Cornerstone, Degreed, Docebo, 360Learning) are adjacent incumbents — included because AI surfaces them as answers to the same buyer questions, though they compete differently than coaching specialists.

03 — THE CONTROLLED COMPARISON

Torch has less brand demand than Valence — and 44% more Share of Model

This is the most useful number in the report, because it controls for brand size. Torch and Valence have effectively identical branded search demand. Torch ties BetterUp — a company with 16× its branded search — at the top of the AI recommendation board. The difference is not awareness, budget, or category position. It's that Torch has published the comparison content the engines now treat as the category's reference text.

WHY

Torch's own comparison pages were the single most-cited source across our 16 answers. When ChatGPT explained what Valence is, it frequently cited Torch — including the framing that classifies Valence as "AI-only." A competitor is currently narrating Valence's category position to the models.

What this proves

Share of Model is not a proxy for brand strength in this category. It is a content-and-citation outcome, and it moves independently of awareness spend. A vendor with Valence's exact brand footprint is already sitting at the top of the board.

The Share of Model gap is closeable without buying awareness. It is a publishing problem.

04 — WHERE VALENCE DISAPPEARS

Named when the buyer says "AI coaching." Absent when the buyer describes a problem.

PERFECT · 5/5

Where the buyer already knows the category

Valence is named in every prompt about enterprise AI-coaching fit ("built for CHROs not small business", "scale to tens of thousands", "SOC 2 / GDPR") and every switching prompt ("alternatives to BetterUp", "alternatives to CoachHub"). If the buyer has already decided they want AI coaching, Valence is on the list.

ZERO · 0/7

Where the buyer describes the problem

Valence is named in none of the use-case or integration prompts: manager effectiveness and performance conversations, leadership development during a restructuring, closing the manager skills gap, driving AI adoption across a workforce, rolling out coaching without more human coaches, "what integrates with Workday and Teams", "we have Workday — what do we add?"

Who gets named instead — and why it matters for the repositioning

On the use-case prompts, the answers go to a completely different vendor set: 15Five, Betterworks, Lattice, Culture Amp, Leapsome for manager effectiveness and performance conversations; Cornerstone, Workday, Degreed, Docebo, 360Learning for talent development at Fortune 500 scale; WalkMe, Whatfix, Prosci, Microsoft Viva for AI adoption and change management. These are exactly the "large established competitors" the new positioning takes on. In the AI recommendation layer, that contest has not started yet — Valence isn't losing it, it isn't being entered into it.

One prompt is worth singling out. Asked "What AI agents or AI coaches do large companies deploy for their employees?" — arguably the most on-thesis question in the set — the answer named Microsoft Copilot, ServiceNow, Workday, Salesforce, SAP and BetterUp. Not Valence.

05 — HOLISTIC SHARE OF SEARCH

Branded search, measured properly

Bare "valence" returns 33,100 searches/mo, which would make Valence look like a category leader. It isn't brand demand — it's chemistry. Following Kantar's holistic method (full keyword long-tail, ambiguity modeling from disambiguated siblings, product→parent mapping for Nadia), Valence's real branded demand is 700–877/mo, or 1.5% share. Note how many names in this category are contaminated: Torch, EZRA, Hone, Lattice, Cloverleaf and Sounding Board all required the same correction.

Three practical consequences

Share of Search is unavailable
The standard brand-health KPI (Binet's Share of Search) cannot be read off the raw token. Any dashboard doing so is reporting chemistry homework.
Search Console becomes the system of record
Only Valence's own GSC is contamination-free, because it counts queries that actually landed on valence.co. We did not have access for this baseline — it's the single highest-value input to add.
Paid search needs auditing for it
A brand or broad-match campaign on "valence" is exposed to a 33,100/mo chemistry pool. Worth confirming match types and negatives against the noise list in the appendix data.

Valence should be measured on Share of Model and first-party organic. Not on branded search volume.

07 — WHEN VALENCE IS NAMED

The repositioning has already landed. It just isn't earning recommendations yet.

We also asked ChatGPT about Valence directly ("is Valence good?", "Valence vs BetterUp", "what is Nadia?", "companies like Valence"). These are excluded from the share tally and read qualitatively. The results are strong — and they isolate the problem to discovery.

STRONG

The new story is in the model

Unprompted, ChatGPT describes Nadia as "becoming a talent-system layer" connecting coaching to "performance reviews, goals, learning, engagement, organizational change." Asked Valence vs BetterUp, it concludes: "I'd put Valence slightly ahead if the objective is workforce-wide talent development." The repositioning language is being repeated accurately.

OWNED CONTENT WORKS

valence.co gets cited — when named

Brand prompts pull valence.co directly and surface real proof: Cushman & Wakefield (Nadia to 10,000 managers in 60 countries), Prudential (9× coaching reach), Delta (−90% review prep time), 100+ enterprise deployments, SOC 2 Type II / ISO 27001 / ISO 42001, Kraft Heinz, Experian, Gilead. The engine can reach this material. It just doesn't reach for it on category questions.

THE ONE SOFT SPOT

Thin independent validation

ChatGPT twice flags that the strongest evidence is Valence's own: "that study is Valence-sponsored, so I'd validate causality independently," and notes Gartner shows only 3 ratings (4.7/5). It also hedges SOC 2 on one category prompt — "verify current certification during diligence" — despite Valence publishing it. Third-party proof is the gap, not the product story.

The framing to watch

Across category answers, Valence is almost always introduced as the "AI-first / AI-only" option, positioned on economics rather than quality — "much lower marginal cost," "best AI-at-scale," "Tier 3 — different economics." That framing comes with a recurring caveat the engines volunteer: "you're giving up relational depth," "not equivalent to a trusted human coach for executive development," "I'd be more cautious for C-suite development, succession, transformational leadership." This is the cheap-scale slot, and it is currently being assigned by competitors' comparison content rather than by Valence's.

08 — WHAT TO DO

Five moves off this data

01

Publish against the problem, not the product

The 0/7 cluster is the whole opportunity. Build substantive pages for the questions CHROs actually ask — manager effectiveness and performance conversations, leading through restructuring, closing the manager skills gap, driving AI adoption at scale — not more pages about AI coaching. That is the territory the repositioning claims and the territory where the engines currently answer "15Five, Betterworks, Cornerstone."

02

Take the comparison surface back from Torch

A competitor's comparison content is the category's reference text, and it is the source assigning Valence the "AI-only, cheap-scale" slot. Own the Valence-vs set directly, and get into the third-party listicles (Risely, Boon, NAVRYN, Cloverleaf, People Managing People) that the engines retrieve. This is the fastest-moving lever in the report.

03

Win the integration questions

Valence scored 0/2 on integration prompts, yet brand answers confirm Teams, Outlook, Copilot, Slack, Google Calendar and Workday integrations exist. CoachHub and Cloverleaf win these answers purely on documentation the engines can read — Workday Marketplace presence, named integration pages. This is a documentation gap, not a product gap, and the cheapest fix here.

04

Get independent proof into the record

The engines explicitly discount Valence-sponsored research and note only 3 Gartner ratings. A review-volume push (Gartner Peer Insights, G2) and one third-party-validated outcome study would directly remove the hedge the models currently attach to Valence's strongest claims.

05

Change what gets reported as brand health

Because the brand token is unmeasurable in public search data, Valence needs a different brand KPI than most B2B companies: Share of Model by journey cluster, plus first-party GSC for branded demand. Lock this run as Baseline Wave 1 and re-run the identical prompt set quarterly — track the cluster pattern, not the decimal, since LLM answers vary run to run while the pattern is stable.

09 — METHODOLOGY

Exactly what we did

Three data pulls, run 2026-10-07, fully repeatable. Built to the Kantar "holistic" standard — full prompt/keyword spectrum, ambiguity correction, product→parent mapping — rather than a naive single-term read.

SHARE OF MODEL

20 prompts × ChatGPT

16 unbranded category prompts (counted toward share) + 4 brand-defensive prompts (qualitative only), run live via the BrightData LLM scraper (web_data_chatgpt_ai_insights). No prompt in the share set names Valence.

SCORING

Mention tally

A brand scores once when named in an answer. A query's own subject is excluded from its tally (BetterUp in "alternatives to BetterUp", CoachHub in "alternatives to CoachHub", Workday in the two Workday prompts). 93 mentions of 37 distinct vendors across 16 answers; share = mentions ÷ 93. Prompts grouped into 5 journey clusters for the heatmap.

SHARE OF SEARCH

Holistic, via DataForSEO

Branded Google volume (US, 12-mo avg) across a 15-brand × 18-modifier long-tail. Ambiguous tokens modeled by attribution from disambiguated siblings — clean category modifiers counted in full, generic modifiers discounted at the same rate as the bare token. Nadia mapped to Valence. Reported as a floor→modeled range.

THE THIRD SURFACE

Google Trends, via DataForSEO

52 weekly points, US, 2025-10-05 → 2026-10-03, normalized 0–100 to each set's peak. Two sets: a competitor-relative core set and a disambiguation set isolating the "valence" token's chemistry contamination. Pearson correlations computed across the 52 weekly points of the disambiguation set.

⚠ Two coverage limits in this baseline — read these before acting on the numbers

1. Single engine. This is a ChatGPT-only run. The Perplexity scraper was hard-blocked on all 17 attempts on 2026-10-07 (sign-up auth wall and upstream 502s), and the Grok scraper is a known timeout. All Share of Model figures are therefore single-engine. In comparable builds the two engines diverge materially — ChatGPT leans on parametric authority, Perplexity retrieves live third-party listicles — so a Perplexity pass could move these numbers meaningfully and should be run before this is treated as a settled baseline. It does not change the cluster pattern, which is the report's main finding, but it could change the leaderboard.

2. No first-party organic. Valence's Google Search Console was not available to us, so the contamination-free read of Valence's own branded demand — normally the most reliable surface in this analysis, and unusually important here given the brand-name ambiguity — is absent. Sections 05 and 06 are modeled from public data only.

Caveats

One engine is a directional read, not a census (see the coverage box above). LLM answers vary run-to-run; the pattern — category-strong / use-case-absent — is stable, the exact mid-pack ordering is not. One prompt ("roll out manager coaching without more human coaches") returned a framework answer naming no vendors at all, and counts as a zero for every brand. Search disambiguation still likely understates the most contaminated names (Sounding Board, Hone, Torch). Single snapshot — re-run quarterly against the identical prompt set.

Why Share of Model matters here specifically

Two things make this the right brand KPI for Valence rather than a nice-to-have. First, branded search is structurally unavailable as a metric because the brand token is dominated by chemistry. Second, Valence sells top-down to CHROs with naturally thin branded search, so the AI recommendation layer is where it is found or skipped. Underlying raw data — all 20 answers, full keyword volumes, both Trends series — is retained and can be expanded into a deck or re-run as Wave 2.
