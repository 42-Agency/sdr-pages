We put 16 unbranded buyer questions a CHRO would actually ask to four AI engines — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity — then measured branded search demand and 12 months of Google Trends. The engines disagree about almost everything in this category except Valence. All four name Valence when the question says "AI coaching." It has not landed in where AI recommends it.

01 — TWO METRICS

Share of Model vs Share of Search

Share of Model = how often a brand is named when a buyer asks an AI engine to recommend a vendor, pooled across all four engines. Share of Search = branded Google demand. Each dot is a vendor: up = recommended more by AI, right = searched more by buyers. This category splits violently. The HR suites (Culture Amp, 15Five, Degreed) own enormous branded search and are barely recommended for coaching. The coaching specialists — Valence, Torch, CoachHub — live in the top-left: strong AI authority, almost no search volume.

This is a good position to be in — Valence is recommended far above its brand size. It also means the AI layer is doing nearly all the discovery work, because there is almost no branded search to fall back on. Which makes Share of Model the KPI that matters here, not search volume.

WHY THIS IS GOOD NEWS

A citation graph this thin and this winnable is unusual. The pages setting the enterprise shortlist are blog posts from vendors a fraction of Valence's size, and no independent analyst source ranks anywhere near the top. There is no entrenched authority to displace — only an empty surface nobody credible has claimed.

What this proves

Share of Model is not a proxy for brand strength in this category — it is a content-and-citation outcome, and it moves independently of awareness spend. Vendors with a fraction of Valence's brand, funding and customer base are outranking it as sources, which is why they outrank it in answers.

The Share of Model gap is closeable without buying awareness. It is a publishing problem.

04 — WHERE VALENCE DISAPPEARS

Named when the buyer says "AI coaching." Absent when the buyer describes a problem.

Grouping the 16 questions by buying-journey stage shows the gap precisely — and running four engines turns it from a pattern into a result. Each cell = how many questions in that cluster that engine answered with Valence. The four engines were given the same questions independently and agree on both halves of the shape.

STRONG · 23/36 WHERE THE CATEGORY IS NAMED

Where the buyer already knows the category

Across discovery, enterprise-fit and switching questions, Valence is named in 23 of 36 engine-answers. Three questions are unanimous across all four engines: "best AI coaching platforms for large enterprises", "software to give every manager in a 50,000-person company coaching", and "AI coaching tools built for enterprise CHROs rather than small business". If the buyer has already decided they want AI coaching, every engine puts Valence on the list.

NEAR-ZERO · 1/28 WHERE IT ISN'T

Where the buyer describes the problem

Across the use-case and integration clusters — 7 questions × 4 engines — Valence is named once. Integration is 0 of 8: not one engine named Valence for "integrates with Workday and Teams" or "we have Workday, what do we add?". Manager effectiveness, restructuring, the manager skills gap and AI-adoption-at-scale are all zero in all four engines.

Who gets named instead — and why it matters for the repositioning

On the use-case questions, every engine routes to a completely different vendor set: 15Five, Betterworks, Lattice, Culture Amp, Leapsome for manager effectiveness and performance conversations; Cornerstone, Workday, SAP SuccessFactors, Degreed, Docebo, 360Learning for talent development at Fortune 500 scale; WalkMe, Whatfix, Prosci, Microsoft Viva for AI adoption and change management. These are exactly the "large established competitors" the new positioning takes on. In the AI recommendation layer, that contest has not started yet — Valence isn't losing it, it isn't being entered into it.

Two questions are worth singling out. "What are the best talent development platforms for Fortune 500 companies?" — the sentence the new positioning is built to win — returned zero mentions of Valence in all four engines, going instead to the HCM suites. And on "What AI agents or AI coaches do large companies deploy for their employees?", three of four engines did name Valence — but ChatGPT answered with Microsoft Copilot, Workday and BetterUp, reading the question as enterprise AI agents rather than coaching.

05 — HOLISTIC SHARE OF SEARCH

Branded search, measured properly

Bare "valence" returns 33,100 searches/mo, which would make Valence look like a category leader. It isn't brand demand — it's chemistry. Following Kantar's holistic method (full keyword long-tail, ambiguity modeling from disambiguated siblings, product→parent mapping for Nadia), Valence's real branded demand is 700–877/mo, or 1.5% share. Note how many names in this category are contaminated: Torch, EZRA, Hone, Lattice, Cloverleaf and Sounding Board all required the same correction.

Three practical consequences

Share of Search is unavailable
The standard brand-health KPI (Binet's Share of Search) cannot be read off the raw token. Any dashboard doing so is reporting chemistry homework.
Search Console becomes the system of record
Only Valence's own GSC is contamination-free, because it counts queries that actually landed on valence.co. We did not have access for this baseline — it's the single highest-value input to add.
Paid search needs auditing for it
A brand or broad-match campaign on "valence" is exposed to a 33,100/mo chemistry pool. Worth confirming match types and negatives against the noise list in the appendix data.

Valence should be measured on Share of Model and first-party organic. Not on branded search volume.

07 — WHEN VALENCE IS NAMED

The repositioning has already landed. It just isn't earning recommendations yet.

We also asked all four engines about Valence directly ("is Valence good?", "Valence vs BetterUp", "what is Nadia?", "companies like Valence") — 16 more answers, excluded from the share tally and read qualitatively. The results are strong, and they isolate the problem precisely: the brand answers are good everywhere the category answers are absent. This is a discovery failure, not a credibility one.

UNANIMOUS YES · 4/4 ENGINES

The new story is in every model

All four engines affirm Valence for the enterprise unprompted. ChatGPT: "a credible shortlist candidate for large enterprises." Gemini: "widely considered one of the top AI-first coaching platforms specifically engineered for large enterprises." Perplexity: "well suited to large enterprises." Gemini volunteers the core differentiator accurately — "unlike generic chatbots or standard LLM wrappers, Valence can ingest an enterprise's specific leadership competency models, cultural values and internal playbooks."

OWNED CONTENT WORKS

valence.co gets read — when Valence is named

Brand questions pull valence.co directly and surface real proof: Delta, Prudential, Experian, Kraft Heinz, General Mills, 100+ enterprise deployments, 1M+ coaching conversations, and the SOC 2 Type II / ISO 27001 / ISO 42001 triple. Claude picked up the strongest single line on the site unaided: "Nadia has passed more than 100 enterprise security reviews, with a 100% success rate." The engines can reach this material. They just don't reach for it on category questions.

THE ONE SOFT SPOT · 4/4 ENGINES

Every engine discounts the evidence

All four attach the same caveat, in their own words. Claude: "most of the evidence comes from Valence itself, so treat it with some caution." ChatGPT: "the published outcomes are vendor-reported… I would recommend a measured pilot." The proof on valence.co is being read and then marked down for being self-sourced. Two engines name the missing piece exactly: ChatGPT found "Gartner Peer Insights currently shows a 4.7/5 rating, but from only three ratings — far too small a sample," and Claude concluded "I found no independent analyst reviews or third-party comparisons, such as Gartner or G2 data." Third-party validation is the gap — not the product story.

The framing to watch — and it is now consistent across all four engines

Every engine introduces Valence as the "AI-first / AI-only" option and positions it on economics rather than quality — "high scale, lower per-seat cost," "more suitable when the goal is broad access," "scalable manager coaching." The same comparison then hands the default to BetterUp. Perplexity states it outright: "For a large enterprise talent program, BetterUp is the safer default." ChatGPT: "I'd default to BetterUp if you need human coaching plus AI in one platform." Gemini frames the choice as "opposite ends of the spectrum," with Valence on cost and BetterUp on depth.

That slot is a direct consequence of section 03. The AI-only classification traces back to competitor comparison tables — Claude cited Torch's by name — and no Valence-authored head-to-head exists to answer it. The cheap-scale slot is currently being assigned by rivals' content, and it is the single framing most at odds with the talent-program repositioning.

08 — WHAT TO DO

Five moves off this data

01

Publish against the problem, not the product

The 1-of-28 cluster is the whole opportunity, and all four engines agree on where it is. Build substantive pages for the questions CHROs actually ask — manager effectiveness and performance conversations, leading through restructuring, closing the manager skills gap, driving AI adoption at scale, talent development at Fortune 500 scale — not more pages about AI coaching. That is the territory the repositioning claims, and the territory where every engine currently answers "15Five, Betterworks, Cornerstone, Workday."

02

Publish the head-to-heads that don't exist

Claude tried to compare Valence and BetterUp and reported the search failed — there was nothing to retrieve. Competitor comparison tables fill that vacuum and assign the "AI-only, cheap-scale" slot. Own the Valence-vs set directly (BetterUp, CoachHub, Torch, EZRA, and "Valence vs staying in Workday"), and get into the roundups the engines actually read: boon-health.com, risely.me, coachello.ai, easygenerator.com, peoplemanagingpeople.com, heypinnacle.com. This is the fastest-moving lever in the report.

03

Win the integration questions

Valence scored 0 of 8 on the integration questions — zero in all four engines — yet the brand answers confirm Teams, Slack, calendar, HCM/HRIS and LMS integrations exist, and Gemini describes them in detail when asked about Valence directly. Cloverleaf wins these answers purely on documentation the engines can read: named integration pages and marketplace listings. This is a documentation gap, not a product gap, and the cheapest fix in the report.

04

Get independent proof into the record

All four engines discount Valence-sourced evidence, and two name the fix: ChatGPT found only 3 Gartner Peer Insights ratings, Claude found no Gartner or G2 data at all. A review-volume push on Gartner Peer Insights and G2, plus one third-party-validated outcome study, would directly remove the hedge every model currently attaches to Valence's strongest claims. Note this is the one gap that content alone cannot close.

05

Change what gets reported as brand health

Because the brand token is unmeasurable in public search data, Valence needs a different brand KPI than most B2B companies: Share of Model by journey cluster, by engine, plus first-party GSC for branded demand. Lock this run as Baseline Wave 1 and re-run the identical 20 prompts across the same four engines quarterly. Track the cluster pattern and the engine agreement, not the decimal — individual answers vary run to run, but a shape four independent engines produce simultaneously is a real market fact, and movement in it is a real result. The two numbers to watch: use-case cells (1/20 today) and valence.co citation share (4 of 64 answers today).

09 — METHODOLOGY

Exactly what we did

Three data pulls, run 2026-10-07, fully repeatable. Built to the Kantar "holistic" standard — full prompt/keyword spectrum, ambiguity correction, product→parent mapping — rather than a naive single-term read.

SHARE OF MODEL

20 prompts × 4 engines = 80 answers

16 unbranded category prompts (counted toward share) + 4 brand-defensive prompts (qualitative only), each run against four engines via the DataForSEO AI Optimization API (/v3/ai_optimization/{engine}/llm_responses/live). Models: gpt-6.1-sol, gemini-3.8-flash, claude-sonnet-5-5, sonar-pro — all with live web-search grounding enabled, so every answer is retrieval-backed and its sources captured. No prompt in the share set names Valence. One pass per engine, no retries; 80/80 calls succeeded.

SCORING

Mention tally

A brand scores once per answer, however many times it is named — so this measures breadth of recommendation, not word count. A question's own subject is excluded from its tally (BetterUp in "alternatives to BetterUp", CoachHub in "alternatives to CoachHub", Workday in the two Workday questions). Brand vocabulary was harvested from the answers themselves rather than authored in advance; chain-of-thought preambles and citation URLs are stripped before matching, so a vendor appearing only in a cited link does not score. 418 mentions of 49 distinct vendors across 64 answers; share = mentions ÷ 418. Questions grouped into 5 journey clusters for the heatmap.

SHARE OF SEARCH

Holistic, via DataForSEO

Branded Google volume (US, 12-mo avg) across a 15-brand × 18-modifier long-tail. Ambiguous tokens modeled by attribution from disambiguated siblings — clean category modifiers counted in full, generic modifiers discounted at the same rate as the bare token. Nadia mapped to Valence. Reported as a floor→modeled range.

THE THIRD SURFACE

Google Trends, via DataForSEO

52 weekly points, US, 2025-10-05 → 2026-10-03, normalized 0–100 to each set's peak. Two sets: a competitor-relative core set and a disambiguation set isolating the "valence" token's chemistry contamination. Pearson correlations computed across the 52 weekly points of the disambiguation set.

⚠ Coverage limits in this baseline — read these before acting on the numbers

1. One pass per engine. Each prompt was run once per engine, not repeated and averaged. LLM answers vary between runs, so a single mid-pack vendor's exact share has real sampling noise around it. Four independent engines agreeing is what makes the pattern trustworthy, not the precision of any one cell — read the cluster shape and the engine agreement, not the decimal. The headline pattern is 23/36 versus 1/28, which is far too large to be sampling noise; the difference between, say, Growthspace at 3.6% and SAP at 3.1% is not.

2. API surface, not consumer UI. These are the engines' production models answering with live web search, accessed via API. They are not pixel-identical to what a CHRO sees in the ChatGPT or Gemini consumer app, which adds personalization, memory and its own retrieval layer. This is the standard trade for a measurable, repeatable baseline, and it is the right trade — but it is a model-layer read, not a screen recording.

3. No first-party organic. Valence's Google Search Console was not available to us, so the contamination-free read of Valence's own branded demand — normally the most reliable surface in this analysis, and unusually important here given the brand-name ambiguity — is absent. Sections 05 and 06 are modeled from public data only.

4. Grok and Copilot not covered. Four engines, not every engine. Grok is not provisioned on our data supplier's account and Microsoft Copilot has no comparable measurement endpoint; both are small shares of enterprise B2B research today, but neither is measured here.

Caveats

The pattern — category-strong, use-case-absent — is stable and replicates independently in all four engines. The exact mid-pack ordering is not; treat anything below the top five as a tie. Three prompts returned framework answers naming no vendors at all ("roll out manager coaching without more human coaches" in three of four engines, and Perplexity's Workday question, which recommended only Workday's own modules) — those count as a zero for every brand, including Valence. Search disambiguation still likely understates the most contaminated names (Sounding Board, Hone, Torch). Single snapshot — re-run quarterly against the identical 20 prompts and the same four models.

Why Share of Model matters here specifically

Two things make this the right brand KPI for Valence rather than a nice-to-have. First, branded search is structurally unavailable as a metric because the brand token is dominated by chemistry. Second, Valence sells top-down to CHROs with naturally thin branded search, so the AI recommendation layer is where it is found or skipped. Underlying raw data — all 80 answers with their 415 captured citations, full keyword volumes, both Trends series — is retained and can be expanded into a deck or re-run as Wave 2.
