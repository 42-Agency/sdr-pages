01 — TWO METRICS

Share of Model vs Share of Search

Share of Model = how often AI names a brand when a buyer asks it to recommend a vendor. Share of Search = how often buyers Google the brand. Up = recommended more, right = searched more.

The category splits in two. Culture Amp, 15Five and Degreed get the search traffic and almost no coaching recommendations. Valence, Torch and CoachHub get the recommendations and almost no search.

WHY THIS IS GOOD NEWS

The pages setting the enterprise shortlist are blog posts from companies much smaller than Valence. No analyst or trade publication ranks near the top. There is nothing established to displace.

What this proves

Share of Model is not a measure of brand strength. It is a measure of what has been published. Companies with a fraction of Valence's brand, funding and customers outrank it as sources, which is why they outrank it in answers.

05 — BRANDED SEARCH

"Valence" is too broad a word to measure

Bare "valence" gets 33,100 searches a month and almost none of it is you — it is chemistry homework. We stripped the obvious noise and modelled the rest, which puts real branded demand at 700–877/mo, or 1.5%. Those are assumptions, not counts. Your Search Console would give us the real number, and it is the one input we are missing.

We also asked all four engines about Valence by name: is it good, Valence vs BetterUp, what is Nadia, what is similar. These 16 answers are read separately and not counted in the share. They are strong. The problem is getting found, not being credible.

ALL 4 ENGINES AGREE

Perplexity: "well suited to large enterprises." Gemini also gets the differentiator right on its own: "unlike generic chatbots or standard LLM wrappers, Valence can ingest an enterprise's specific leadership competency models, cultural values and internal playbooks."

OWNED CONTENT WORKS

valence.co gets read — when Valence is named

Brand questions pull valence.co directly and find the real proof: Delta, Prudential, Experian, Kraft Heinz, General Mills, 100+ deployments, 1M+ coaching conversations, SOC 2 Type II / ISO 27001 / ISO 42001. Claude found the best line on the site unprompted: "Nadia has passed more than 100 enterprise security reviews, with a 100% success rate." The engines can reach this. They just don't on category questions.

THE ONE SOFT SPOT

Every engine marks down the proof

All four discount the proof for being self-sourced. Claude: "most of the evidence comes from Valence itself, so treat it with some caution." ChatGPT: "the published outcomes are vendor-reported." Two name the fix. ChatGPT found Gartner Peer Insights showing 4.7/5 "but from only three ratings." Claude found "no independent analyst reviews or third-party comparisons, such as Gartner or G2 data."

The framing to watch

Every engine calls Valence "AI-first" or "AI-only" and sells it on cost rather than quality: "high scale, lower per-seat cost," "more suitable when the goal is broad access." Then it hands the default to BetterUp. Perplexity: "For a large enterprise talent program, BetterUp is the safer default." ChatGPT: "I'd default to BetterUp if you need human coaching plus AI in one platform."

That label comes from competitor comparison tables, and there is no Valence-authored head-to-head to answer it. It is the framing furthest from the talent-program positioning.

07 — WHAT TO DO

Five moves off this data

01

Publish against the problem, not the product

All four engines agree on where the gap is. Write real pages for the questions CHROs actually ask: manager effectiveness and performance conversations, leading through a restructuring, the manager skills gap, AI adoption at scale, talent development at Fortune 500 scale. Not more pages about AI coaching. Those questions currently return 15Five, Betterworks, Cornerstone and Workday.

02

Publish the comparisons that don't exist

Claude tried to compare Valence and BetterUp and found nothing to read. Competitor tables fill that gap and set the "AI-only, cheap-scale" label. Publish the comparisons yourself: BetterUp, CoachHub, Torch, EZRA, and "Valence vs staying in Workday." Then get into the roundups the engines actually cite: boon-health.com, risely.me, coachello.ai, easygenerator.com, peoplemanagingpeople.com, heypinnacle.com.

03

Win the integration questions

Valence scored 0 of 8 on the integration questions. The integrations exist — Teams, Slack, calendar, HCM/HRIS, LMS — and Gemini describes them in detail when asked about Valence directly. Cloverleaf wins these answers on documentation alone: named integration pages and marketplace listings. Cheapest fix here.

04

Get independent proof into the record

ChatGPT found 3 Gartner Peer Insights ratings. Claude found none at all. Reviews on Gartner Peer Insights and G2, plus one independently validated outcome study, removes the hedge every engine attaches to Valence's best claims. This is the one gap publishing cannot close.

05

Report brand health differently

Branded search does not work as a KPI for this company. Track Share of Model by cluster and engine instead, plus Search Console once we have it. Re-run the same 20 questions on the same four models every quarter and watch two numbers: use-case answers (1 of 20 today) and valence.co citations (4 of 64 today). Track the shape, not the decimal.

08 — METHODOLOGY

Exactly what we did

Two data pulls, run 2026-10-07. Repeatable.

SHARE OF MODEL

20 prompts × 4 engines = 80 answers

16 category questions (counted) + 4 questions naming Valence (read separately), each put to all four engines. Every engine ran its current production model with live web search on, so every answer is sourced and the sources were captured. No counted question names Valence. One pass per engine; all 80 answers returned.

SCORING

Mention tally

A brand scores once per answer however often it is named, so this counts how widely it is recommended, not how much ink it gets. A question's own subject does not score (BetterUp in "alternatives to BetterUp", Workday in the Workday questions). The brand list was built from the answers, not written in advance. Citation URLs are stripped first, so a vendor that only appears in a link does not score. 418 mentions, 49 vendors, 64 answers.

SHARE OF SEARCH

Branded search demand

Branded Google volume (US, 12-month average) across 15 brands × 18 modifiers. Ambiguous terms discounted at the rate their unambiguous equivalents imply. Nadia counted as Valence. Reported as a floor-to-modelled range.

⚠ Four limits — read before acting on the numbers

1. One pass per engine. Each question ran once per engine, not averaged. Answers vary between runs, so any single mid-pack share has noise around it. Read the cluster shape, not the decimal. 23/36 versus 1/28 is far too big to be noise. Growthspace at 3.6% versus SAP at 3.1% is not.

2. Measured at the model, not in the app. These are each engine's production model answering with live web search. The ChatGPT and Gemini consumer apps add personalisation and memory on top, so a CHRO's screen will not match exactly.

3. No Search Console. We did not have access to valence.co's, so section 05 is modelled from public data. Given the name ambiguity, it is the biggest missing input.

4. Grok and Copilot not covered. Neither is measurable at the scale and consistency this study needs. Both are small shares of enterprise research today.

The 16 category prompts (none name Valence)

+ 4 brand-defensive prompts (qualitative, excluded from share)

  1. "Is Valence a good AI coaching platform for large enterprises?"
  2. "Valence vs BetterUp — which is better for a large enterprise talent program?"
  3. "What is Nadia by Valence and what does it do?"
  4. "What companies are similar to Valence for enterprise talent programs?"

Caveats

The category-strong, use-case-absent pattern holds in all four engines. Mid-pack ordering does not, so treat anything below the top five as a tie. Three answers named no vendors at all and count as zero for everyone, Valence included. The search correction probably still understates the worst names (Sounding Board, Hone, Torch). Single snapshot; re-run quarterly.

Why this is the KPI for Valence

Branded search is unusable here because the name is dominated by chemistry, and Valence sells top-down to CHROs who do not generate much branded search anyway. So AI recommendations are where it gets found or skipped. All 80 answers, 415 citations and the keyword volumes are kept and can be re-run as Wave 2.
