THE BOTTOM LINE

Run the pilot. Change what it buys.

1. The organic position is strong — and it changes the goal.

ChatGPT names Steno unprompted in 16 of 23 pilot conversations, including 4 out of 4 plain "best court reporting agency" questions and 8 of 9 competitor-alternative questions. The pilot is not buying visibility Steno lacks. It is buying a second placement in conversations Steno already wins — which is a reasonable thing to do, but it should be measured as incremental lift, not as first contact.

2. A third of the keyword list doesn't exist.

"Court reporting in Atlanta", "in Los Angeles", "in San Francisco", "in Dallas", "in Fort Worth", "in San Antonio", "West Coast" — all zero monthly searches. Same for most of the "alternatives to / similar to / substitute" long-tail. On StackAdapt, which runs exact-match, those lines deliver literally nothing.

3. The head term is a budget trap.

court reporter carries 14,800 of Theme A's 20,610 monthly searches — 72% of it — at $50.83 a click. It's also the most intent-ambiguous term on the list: a large share of people searching it want to become a court reporter, not hire one. At $25/day it returns about 14 clicks over four weeks. It cannot produce a readable result.

4. Deposition summary software is the wrong pond.

The entire theme is 440 searches a month. And it is the one place Steno has no organic standing — ChatGPT names Everlaw, Relativity, LexisNexis, Verbit and Parrot instead (Steno 1 of 4). Small demand, and a fight Steno isn't currently in. Not a pilot theme.

5. Conquest is where the pilot is affordable.

Competitor terms average $6.94 a click against $43.15 for the core court-reporting set. The same $700 buys roughly 140 clicks instead of 14 — ten times the learning. Planet Depos (2,400/mo) and Lexitas Legal (1,300/mo) carry real volume.

So what do we do?

Back the brief's own call — go direct ChatGPT, not StackAdapt — and we now have a second reason: StackAdapt's exact-match model would spend against a keyword list that is a third dead. Then narrow the pilot to conquest + the five geo terms that actually have volume, drop court reporter as a pilot term, and drop the deposition-software theme entirely. Same $700, roughly ten times the data.

01 — THE ORGANIC BASELINE

Before you buy the conversation, check whether you're already in it

We turned each of the pilot's three targeting themes into the conversations a real buyer would have, and ran all 23 live through ChatGPT — the surface the ads will appear on. Then we counted who ChatGPT names. This tells you where an ad adds a placement Steno doesn't already have, and where it simply doubles up.

✓ ALREADY WINNING — ADS REINFORCE

Discovery (4/4) and competitor conquest (8/9)

Ask ChatGPT "what is the best court reporting agency?" and Steno is named — every time, in all four phrasings. Ask for alternatives to Veritext, Lexitas, U.S. Legal Support, Esquire, Magna, Planet Depos, Huseby or Victory, and Steno is named in eight of the nine.

The single miss is Accurate Court Reporters — where ChatGPT names Veritext, U.S. Legal Support, Esquire, Planet Depos and Huseby, but not Steno. That term has 10 searches a month, so it is not worth fixing with budget.

⚠ NOT IN THE ROOM — ADS WOULD BE FILLING A REAL GAP

Three states, and deposition software

Steno is not named when the buyer asks for court reporting in Atlanta / Georgia, New York, or Texas. ChatGPT reaches for Veritext, U.S. Legal Support, Magna and NAEGELI instead. It is named for California, the West Coast, and the plain "certified court reporter" question.

This mirrors the coverage objection the models raise elsewhere: Steno reads as a California-first agency. That is the genuine organic hole — and, usefully, New York (260/mo), Texas (110/mo) and Houston (320/mo) are among the few geo terms with real demand.

The pilot is $25/day for four weeks on each route — $700. That budget is fine. What it's aimed at is not. At the head term's cost per click, the pilot ends with a sample too small to read; at conquest prices, it ends with a real result.

04 — RECOMMENDED SPEND MAP

Same $700. Ten times the learning.

Three changes to the plan as written. The route recommendation in the brief stands — go direct.

01

Lead with conquest — ~60% of budget

It's ten times cheaper per click, it has real volume (Planet Depos 2,400/mo, Lexitas Legal 1,300/mo, Esquire and Veritext 590 each), and Steno already wins the organic answer 8 times out of 9 — so the ad lands in a conversation where the recommendation is already in Steno's favour. That's the highest-converting placement available. Drop the zero-volume "similar to / substitute" tail; keep the brand terms.

02

Buy the three states Steno is losing — ~30%

New York (260/mo), Texas + Houston (430/mo combined) and Georgia (90/mo) are the only geo terms with both real demand and an organic gap — ChatGPT does not name Steno in any of them. This is the one part of the pilot buying something Steno doesn't already have. Skip Atlanta, LA, SF, Dallas, Fort Worth, San Antonio and "West Coast": zero volume.

03

Hold back court reporter — ~10% as a probe

Don't make the head term the pilot. At $50.83 it eats the budget and returns 14 clicks. Worse, its intent is split — a large share of that 14,800 is people who want to become a court reporter. Run it as a small capped probe purely to observe ChatGPT's real clearing price, then decide with data. If the pilot proves out, this is a phase-two term with a phase-two budget.

04

Cut deposition summary software entirely

440 searches a month across the whole theme, and the only theme where Steno has no organic standing — ChatGPT names Everlaw, Relativity, LexisNexis, Verbit and Parrot. Fighting for a slot Steno doesn't hold, in a pond with no water, on a $700 pilot. It's the clearest cut on the board. Revisit it as a content problem, not a media one.

And measure it as incremental, not as discovery

This is the measurement point that follows from the organic baseline. Steno is already named in 16 of the 23 target conversations. If the pilot reports conversions from those conversations and books them as "ChatGPT ads worked," it will be taking credit for demand that was already arriving. Lock this baseline now, before launch — then the honest question at the end of four weeks is not "did we get conversions?" but "did we get more than we were already getting for free?" That is the only version of this test worth running.

05 — HOW WE TESTED IT

Method

ORGANIC BASELINE

23 conversations, ChatGPT only

We rewrote the brief's three keyword themes as the questions a buyer would actually ask, and ran all 23 live through ChatGPT (BrightData scraper), because ChatGPT is the surface the ads appear on. Then we recorded every vendor named in each answer. Steno: 16 of 23.

DEMAND + PRICE

All 65 keywords, DataForSEO

US monthly search volume and cost-per-click for every keyword in the brief, pulled live. Volumes and CPCs are Google's — the closest available proxy, and exact for the StackAdapt route, which runs on the same exact-match model.

WHAT THIS IS NOT

Caveats

ChatGPT ad auction prices aren't public — treat CPCs as ratios, not forecasts. LLM answers vary between runs; we re-ran to confirm the pattern holds. And ChatGPT matches on topics, not exact keywords, so a zero-volume keyword may still see conversations — it just can't be verified, and can't run on StackAdapt.

One thing worth knowing about how you phrase the question

In the companion Share of Model report we asked ChatGPT longer, enterprise-flavoured discovery questions — "what do AmLaw 100 litigation teams use", "a national provider for multi-state litigation", "a mid-size firm with a heavy deposition calendar" — and Steno was named almost never. Here, asking the short commercial question the ads actually target — "what is the best court reporting agency?" — Steno is named every time.

Both are true, and together they say something useful: Steno holds the plain shortlist and loses the enterprise one. The moment a buyer signals scale — national coverage, multi-state, AmLaw — ChatGPT reaches for Veritext and U.S. Legal Support instead. That's a credibility gap, not an awareness gap, and it is not something ad budget fixes. It is worth knowing before anyone reads a pilot result as a verdict on the brand.
