THE BOTTOM LINE — IN PLAIN ENGLISH

Steno sells technology. The market has filed it under "the one that lets you pay later."

1. The AI recall is real, and it's top-four.

Across 32 answers, Steno was named 9 times — 4th of 18 vendors, ahead of U.S. Legal Support, Planet Depos and Magna. For a company far smaller than the incumbents, that's a genuine asset. This is not a visibility problem.

2. But Steno is never on the default shortlist.

Ask "who are the best court reporting agencies?" or "who do large firms use?" — the four ways a buyer opens the search — and Steno is named zero times out of eight. Lexitas, Veritext and Esquire take those slots every time.

3. It gets named for money, not for technology.

Steno appears on the deferred-payment questions (DelayPay) and on the "who's an alternative to Veritext?" questions. It does not appear on any of the six questions about remote-deposition technology, exhibit sharing or realtime transcript — which is what steno.com leads with.

4. Nobody finds Steno by searching the category.

Steno's own Search Console shows every one of its top 15 organic queries is someone typing the brand name. Not a single category term — "court reporting agency", "deposition services" — earns a click. Organic is growing fast (+283% year on year), but it's all people who already knew the name.

5. The name itself is working against the brand.

"Steno" is a common word — steno pad, steno machine, stenography. Most people typing it are not looking for the company. That makes branded search a weak signal for Steno specifically, and it's why we lean on Search Console, which only counts people who actually reached steno.com.

So what do we do?

Two moves. (1) Bank the win: the challenger slot is real and defensible — lean into "the alternative to Veritext" and DelayPay, which is the one thing the models already give Steno for free. (2) Close the gap: Steno's technology claim exists on its website and nowhere else the models can see it. Publish the proof — realtime transcript, exhibit sharing, Transcript Genius — where AI actually reads it, or keep losing the technology question to vendors with worse technology.

The rest of this report shows the evidence behind each point, across four independent surfaces: AI answers, Google search demand, search interest over time, and Steno's own Search Console.

⚠ THE MISMATCH

Steno's homepage claim gets zero AI recall

steno.com leads with technology — Steno Connect for Zoom, realtime transcript, exhibit sharing, Transcript Genius. A testimonial on the homepage says Steno's "technology continues to outshine any other court reporting agency."

We asked both engines six separate questions about exactly that: best platform for remote depositions, which agencies have the best remote-deposition technology, what lets attorneys share exhibits and see realtime transcript in a Zoom deposition. Steno was named zero times out of six. Veritext, Lexitas and LiveLitigation took those answers instead.

✓ THE SLOT STENO DOES OWN

DelayPay and "the alternative to Veritext"

Where Steno does get named is money and challenger positioning. On "which agencies let firms pay after the case settles?" ChatGPT names Steno directly. On "what are good alternatives to Veritext and U.S. Legal Support?" both engines name Steno.

DelayPay has done something Steno's technology marketing has not: it gave the models a specific, differentiated reason to name the brand. It is the sharpest asset Steno has in AI, and it is currently doing all the work.

The gap nobody is filling: Perplexity won't name anyone for deferred payment

Asked how a contingency-fee plaintiff firm can defer deposition costs, Perplexity gave a long, careful, entirely vendor-free answer — it explains that you should "use deferred-payment plans with court reporting vendors" and then names not one of them. Same on the cost question. That is an open slot on the single topic Steno is already closest to owning: the engine is describing DelayPay's category and failing to name DelayPay. That is a content problem, and it is fixable.

HOW WE CORRECTED FOR THE AMBIGUITY — AND WHY 4.0% IS STILL TOO LOW

The honest range is 4% to roughly 10%

We can't just count the 14,800 monthly searches for steno as Steno's — most aren't. We also can't throw them away, which would zero out the brand. So we estimate the brand-attributable slice from the disambiguated siblings: people searching steno court reporting / steno depositions / steno login versus steno pad / steno machine / stenography. That model gives Steno 4.0%, and it is the floor.

Steno's own Search Console then tells us the model is too conservative. steno.com actually pulls roughly 5,000 clicks a month from the bare word steno alone — several times more brand demand than our model was willing to credit it with. Corrected against that first-party evidence, Steno's true Share of Search is closer to 9–10%, which would place it 4th rather than 7th — the same rank it holds in AI. We report both numbers rather than pick the flattering one.

08 — WHEN WE ASK ABOUT STENO BY NAME

The reputation is excellent. The problem is discovery, not perception.

We asked both engines directly about Steno — is it good, how does it compare to Veritext and Lexitas, what do reviews say, what is DelayPay. When prompted by name, the models describe Steno accurately and favourably. There is no misperception to correct here. The entire gap is getting surfaced in the first place, when the buyer hasn't said the name.

STRONG

Reviews are a genuine asset

The engines consistently surface Steno's ~4.9-star G2 standing and describe it as the tech-forward, service-strong option. When Steno is in the conversation, it wins the comparison. It just isn't in enough conversations.

THE ASSET

DelayPay is understood correctly

Both engines explain deferred payment accurately and tie it to Steno specifically. This is the one concept the models have genuinely learned — and it's why Steno wins the cost cluster. It is a repeatable pattern: give the models a specific, ownable idea and they will attach the brand to it.

THE HONEST KNOCK

Coverage doubts

The recurring caveat is geographic reach — the engines position the national incumbents as the safe multi-state choice and Steno as strongest in California. That objection is worth pre-empting directly in content if the coverage story has changed.

09 — WHAT TO DO

Four moves off this data

01

Publish the technology proof where the models can read it

Steno's technology claim currently lives on steno.com and nowhere else — which is why it earns 0/6 on the technology questions. The engines answer those questions from third-party pages, comparison articles and review sites. Get specific, checkable capability content — realtime transcript, exhibit sharing in Zoom, Transcript Genius — onto those surfaces. This is the single highest-leverage gap in the report.

02

Take the deferred-payment slot outright

Perplexity explains deferred payment for depositions in detail and names no vendor at all. That is an unclaimed answer on the topic Steno is already closest to owning. Publish the definitive, citable resource on how contingency-fee firms finance deposition costs — and DelayPay becomes the default answer rather than an unnamed concept.

03

Get onto the shortlist lists

Steno is 0/8 on "who are the best court reporting agencies" — the question that opens every buying process. Those answers are drawn almost entirely from "best court reporting services" roundups, where Lexitas, Veritext and Esquire appear and Steno often doesn't. This is a tractable, well-understood placement job, and it moves the biggest cluster on the board.

04

Stop measuring brand with the word "steno"

Any brand-tracking that counts searches for steno is counting stenography students. Track Search Console clicks on branded queries plus this Share of Model instead — both are clean. Lock this run as the baseline and re-run the identical prompt set quarterly; watch the cluster shape, not the decimal.

THE AMBIGUITY CORRECTION — AND A CORRECTION WE MADE TO OUR OWN METHOD

Why "parrot pricing" is not a legal-tech search

When a brand's name is a common word, you cannot count its bare token as brand demand — but you also cannot count steno pricing or parrot app, because a modifier like "pricing" doesn't disambiguate anything. Our first pass made exactly that mistake and credited Parrot, the legal-transcription startup, with 6,935 monthly searches — most of which were people looking for the bird and the drone company. It ranked Parrot above Steno. Corrected, Parrot has ~717.

The fix, applied throughout: split the modifiers into those that carry category meaning ("steno court reporting" — can only mean the brand, counted in full) and those that don't ("steno pricing", bare "steno" — counted only at the brand-attributable fraction estimated from the disambiguated siblings). Steno's floor is 4.0%; corrected against Search Console it is nearer 9–10%. We show both.

Two engines is a directional read, not a census — the cluster shape is the signal, not any single decimal. Why this matters for Steno

Vendor discovery is moving from Google to AI assistants, and Steno's brand token is too contaminated for conventional branded-search tracking to work. That makes the AI recommendation layer both the place Steno is found or skipped, and one of the few clean instruments available to measure it.
