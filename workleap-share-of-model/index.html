AI rates Workleap higher than the market searches for it

Workleap sits in the top-left pocket with Leapsome: recommended near the leaders, searched like a challenger. Bonusly and Motivosity are the mirror image — big search footprints from daily users, thin AI presence. Lattice is the only brand holding both.

03 — WHAT AI READS

The engines' reading list is written by your competitors

Engines read pages, then recommend whoever those pages talk about — so whoever wrote the pages writes the shortlist. Every answer came with its sources and we captured all of them: 148 distinct domains. The most-cited vendor site in the category is Betterworks' blog, in 11 of 64 answers. Workleap's site appears in 3 — and every one is a help-center article, not a page built to win a comparison.

Most-cited sources across the 64 answers

Counted once per answer. Gemini hides its publishers behind a redirect, so those 7 are excluded rather than guessed at.

THE SHORT VERSION

Of the 14 most-cited domains, 10 are competitor-owned blogs — Betterworks, Achievers, Eletive, Matter, Teamflect and others publishing "best engagement software" roundups that rank the author first. The pages deciding the AI shortlist are competitor marketing, and Workleap isn't on that shelf.

WHY THIS CONNECTS TO YOUR BLOG

Workleap's organic traffic comes overwhelmingly from inspirational-quote and definition posts. That content earns clicks but is never cited on a buying question — it answers the wrong questions. The category pages engines actually read, competitors wrote.

THE GOOD NEWS

No analyst gatekeeper controls this. The sources are blog posts, and the price of entry is publishing better ones — problem-framed, citable category pages. That is a content decision, not a Magic Quadrant cycle.

AI recommends whoever published, not whoever is biggest.

05 — BRANDED SEARCH

The brand people still search for is Pingboard

Branded search is the market telling you which names it remembers. Workleap's total is 5,100 searches a month — 6.2% of the category, #6 of 10. And inside that total, the acquired brands still carry it: Pingboard gets 2,400/mo and "workleap" itself 1,300. Three years after the rebrand, the legacy name is searched almost twice as often as the master brand. Culture Amp's single name gets 14,800/mo — eleven times Workleap's.

Share of branded search — US, 12-month average

Each brand measured on its full name + modifier basket. Workleap includes Officevibe, Pingboard, GSoft and Didacte. Lattice and Achievers share their names with other things; both are modelled down to in-category demand.

The quiet signal: login searches

People Google "[brand] login" when they use a product daily. Bonusly gets 3,600 of those a month, Lattice 2,900 — Workleap gets 70. Competitors' end users generate constant brand exposure inside their own workday. That compounding loop is what Workleap's share numbers are up against.

08 — METHODOLOGY

Exactly what we did

SHARE OF MODEL

21 prompts × 4 engines = 84 answers

16 category questions (counted) + 5 naming Workleap (read separately), each put to all four engines on their current production models with live web search on. No counted question names Workleap. Every answer and its sources were kept, and the tally was independently re-checked answer by answer.

SCORING

Mention tally

A brand scores once per answer, so this counts how widely it is recommended, not how much ink it gets. Officevibe and Pingboard mentions count as Workleap. A question's own subject doesn't score (Culture Amp in "alternatives to Culture Amp"). The vendor list was built from the answers, not written in advance. 433 mentions, 33 vendors, 64 answers.
